What the model does

  • Pitch follows firmware-spec §2: the mirror moves by −err/2 + aim_down/2, clamped to ±7.5°.
  • With the lidar fitted, the road estimate blends 70% toward the slope it measures over the next 12 m, and ignores anything steeper than +12% (§8).
  • Hill follower (firmware-spec §2, adopted 2026-11-21) removes the pitch the measured acceleration explains, then follows the road with a 0.3 s time constant, so a grade change settles in under 1 s. It freezes for 0.3 s after anything harder than 1 m/s². Tick “Old hill follower” to compare with the earlier 4 s version.
  • The wheel pitches up to ±10° when accelerating or braking (the balance angle for 1.7 m/s²). The servo moves the mirror at about 150°/s.
  • Head 0.8 m above the road, cutoff 1.2° down, so it meets a flat road about 38 m ahead.
  • Yaw limit from the CAD model: each beam swings up to 25° outward, through the clear wraparound side. With this rule no beam ever swings inward.

What it assumes

  • Yaw: the beam on the turn side swings into the curve and the other stays straight (owner, 2026-11-21). The swinging beam aims at the road 20 m ahead along the curve (yaw ≈ 20 m × yaw rate ÷ 2 × speed), up to 25°, rate-limited to 90°/s. The exact rule isn't in the firmware spec yet.
  • Leaning is corrected by tilting each mirror so the middle of its cutoff sits where it would with the wheel upright. A tilt is left within each half.
  • The IMU reading is perfect. The real gated filter adds a small lag and noise.
  • Servo speed, mirror ringing and the cutoff's compound-angle error are not modelled.
